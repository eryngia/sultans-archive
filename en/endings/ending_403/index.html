Witcher's Codex - Entry A-208 Name: [player.name] Titles: Coitus Web, Demon Prince, Patron of Adultery, Master of the 1036th Layer of the Abyss, God-Slayer Rank: Abyssal Lord Common Bestowals: Shedding, Extra Limbs, Venom Fangs, Mucous Secretion, Sensory Delusions Activity: Actively Lures Mortals This young Abyssal Lord enjoys widespread mortal worship - unsurprising, given his ability to seduce shallow devotees through base sensory pleasures in dreams. Invoking his titles, sigils, or summoning beacons amplifies sensory and hallucinatory magics within his domain. [Warning! Such acts risk drawing his direct attention. Never take it lightly! (See Appendix III: The Green Tower Incident)] The Coitus Web lacks formal churches. His cultists parasitize other congregations - often those of true gods or potent astral entities. They corrupt high-spiritual-awareness devotees through depraved rituals, or secretly breed "promising seeds" via adulterous unions if existing bloodlines prove inadequate (See Appendix II: Queenie). When conditions align, an infant of supreme spiritual awareness is conceived through unholy congress steeped in deceit - embodying this Demon Prince's core doctrine: Lust is the web that ensnares souls. Documented cases (Appendices I, II, IV) reveal these tainted infants become conduits for divine essence, even luring godly avatars into the material plane. Foolish deities mistake this for opportunity, only to meet the Abyssal Lord's fangs. Cultists instantly sacrifice the infant to the Abyss, their prolonged infiltration and blasphemies having already forged the gateway. The ensuing deicide triggers cascading calamities - earthquakes, plagues, mass casualties - demanding proactive intervention despite its moral quandaries. Disrupting incomplete rituals can thwart this Lord's schemes. Note: All contaminated lifeforms, including infants and beasts, must be purged without mercy. Even decades-old rituals may resurrect (See Appendix II). Given his expertise in corruption over combat, forging pacts with this entity is strongly advised against. Long-term peril far outweighs fleeting gains.
