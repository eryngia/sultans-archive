Students from the capital gather around her shrine, carried by eight sturdy men, to pay their respects. It is believed that if one dreams of Lumera gently touching their forehead with her fingertip on the eve of an exam, success is guaranteed. Then Manar's shrine arrives. Similar to Lumera's shrine, Manar's shrine has no statue but a map, with many regions shrouded in shadow-like clouds. Legend has it that the map expands each year as Manar, the Goddess of Journeys, continues to create new realms to this day. Next in line is the statue of Jenna, one of the most revered deities at the ceremony! Her exquisite statue, adorned with dazzling jewels, captivates onlookers, while her priests extract handfuls of gem fragments—agate, jade pieces, crystal—from their garments, scattering them over the crowd. Refracting the brilliant sunlight, these gems are creations that goddess Jenna takes immense pride in. Up next are the statues of Samir and Kapar. These two statues share a modest shrine, depicted only as crude silhouettes—eyeglasses, water containers, and hands laden with plants. Their priests distribute remedies for bruises and stomach discomfort along the street. It is widely believed that these remedies, received on this day, ensure relief from such ailments for the entire year, prompting everyone to eagerly grab a handful. Following closely is the statue of Faraj, the God of Elements. His priests are adorned in magnificent attire, accompanied by a group of sixteen men who bear his shrine. This extraordinary deity, the most steadfast disciple of the Supreme God [player.name], is revered for creating fundamental elements such as air and water. Faraj also holds the esteemed position of being the patron deity of the king's servants, as every minister devoutly follows his teachings. Oh, and here comes the statue of Lady Becky! As this adorable statue appears, joyful cheers fill the air. Whether they are mule traders, cat vendors, owners of dog tracks, or sellers of live poultry, all come together in anticipation—Lady Becky is renowned for bestowing blessings upon the well-being of animals. It is worth noting that his shrine only accepts offerings of dried fish and chicken strips! As expected, the shrine of Lady Malkina now arrives, shrouded in layers of intricately colored gauze curtains, exuding an indescribably complex fragrance. Many rush to toss coins into the shrine, hoping to attract her favor. Indeed, for this Goddess of Countenance, enhancing one's appearance and allure seems like the simplest of tasks. Ah, the arrival of Nawfal's shrine! It bears no statue, only a stone slab engraved with numerous deep grooves. When one wishes to challenge fate, they stand before this slab to make a vow to Nawfal, the guardian of rebels. Should their defiance prove successful, they return to the slab to leave a carved mark. Throughout the centuries, the stone slabs have been repeatedly replaced—the courage of rebels has never wavered. Inal's statue is quite unassuming—light enough for a young lady to carry on her shoulders. She delicately carries the goddess's statue through the procession, occasionally reaching into her bosom to retrieve packets of pomegranate seeds to share with the children lining the road. Legend has it that those who consume these seeds will become nimble, capable of aiding their parents with chores, and assisting each other in combing their hair. Now, the statue of the Golden Lady makes its grand entrance—exquisitely crafted, lavishly adorned, and incredibly heavy! Originally known as Zazie, this Goddess of Ambition earned the moniker "Golden Lady" after insisting in a priest's dream divination that "my statue must be made of pure gold." Her temple is a constant hub of activity, with a steady stream of believers flocking in, mirroring humanity's ever-expanding ambitions. Following the Golden Lady's statue is that of Qais, carried by an elderly man donning a simple gray robe, with white hair and beard. He bows his head, supporting Qais's unadorned statue on his shoulder. Qais, the patron deity of righteousness, requires no lavish offerings in his sanctuaries. He often reveals himself to intervene in mortal affairs, earning deep respect and reverence. Next in line are the priests of Iman, not bearing Iman's statue but carrying bottles filled with jasmine perfume, which they sprinkle along the streets. As the Recorder of All Deities, Iman has no temple of his own, yet all other temples are erected under the guidance of his priests. He holds the responsibility of transcribing the words of the Supreme God. The followers of Iman are few in number, likely aligning with his own wishes. Following them are the priests of Gullis, the God of the Hunt. Although this god does not require sculptures or shrines, every hunter's bow and arrow proudly bear his name engraved. The priests chant hunting hymns, causing flocks of birds to take flight from rooftops. Then, the priests of Habib, the God of Cooking, make their grand appearance! By the way, Habib is among the most beloved deities! With radiant smiles, the priests gleefully distribute delicious jerky to the eager individuals waiting by the roadside, the recipe for which remains a closely guarded secret. The shrine of Jabal, the God of War, is ceremoniously carried by twelve priests. Initially revered as the God of Victory, he later transitioned to become the God of Combat and now holds the title of the "God of War"— overseeing both realms of combat and warfare. His divine influence and followers continue to grow. Interestingly, Jabal's priests refrain from bathing, making it easy for all to find their temple by following the distinct pungent scent. In contrast to Jabal's grand shrine, the shrine of Adila, the Goddess of Valor, appears notably humble. She governs the innate courage of all souls, igniting a fervor within her followers' hearts that instills unwavering determination to overcome any challenge. The priestesses of Adila, all skilled warrioresses named "Adila," rarely need to speak on behalf of their deity—those in need always find solace in seeking their aid, and during city fires, they are frequently seen involved in rescue operations. Next, the shrine of Badriyyah emerges—a stone-carved door tightly sealed and adorned with runes. Despite the limited worship of this goddess, the priests of Iman diligently maintain her offerings, believing that without her protection at the portal, the world would be overrun by dreadful demons... Alim, the God of Theft, has now made his presence felt! The crowd instinctively tightens their grip on their wallets. This deity not only patronizes all thieves but also masters every deceitful tactic known. Despite the homage paid to him, his shrine often appears deserted. Following Alim, the crowd witnesses the shrine of Riel, the Slave Liberator. It is a simple wooden shrine bearing a severed shackle, symbolizing her role in protecting all enslaved souls. However, once freed, these former slaves no longer receive favor from this goddess. "Freed people must earn their livelihood through their own efforts," is the personal directive she imparted to her priests. The shrine of Mahir, the Goddess of Creation, comes into view. This unique shrine doesn't require priests to carry it—it moves autonomously. Specifically, it is a massive wooden barrel equipped with tracks that propel it forward... The crowd cautiously keeps its distance from its path. Mahir's devotees unveil a new invention at the Supreme God Festival each year, and this year's creation shows no signs of explosion... so far. Approaching next are the priests of Asal, four of them collectively carrying a stone slab engraved with two lines: Entry 1: The Creator, generally referring to [player.name]. Entry 2: Knowledge, referring to humanity's quest for a comprehensive understanding of the world created by the Creator. Legend has it that these were the initial two lines inscribed by Asal after his inception. Therefore, all those seeking knowledge and wisdom must quietly recite these lines, beseeching Asal's guidance. Following Asal's shrine is that of Fardak. This bronze shrine is adorned with numerous golden stars, representing Fardak's greatest feat: the creation of the starry sky with all constellations, including the constellation of Lumera, his beloved. "Make way! Clear a path, everyone!" Following closely are several immensely popular deities. Once again, the crowd is compelled to part ways to allow their grand processions to pass. First among them is Nabhani, the patron of lovers and swordsmen. His priests, handsome youths clad in rose-red robes with meticulously styled hair, bear a statue strikingly resembling Nabhani himself. Legend has it that this deity has appeared in the dreams of his priests multiple times, meticulously guiding them in every aspect of the carving—sharper nose, more defined wrists, and deeper-set eyes... Devotees seeking love flock to touch the statue, overwhelming the priests' attempts to restrain them, resulting in a chaotic scene. Nabhani's popularity is perhaps rivaled only by Jawad and his daughter Arumina. Jawad, revered as the God of Fortune, and Arumina, the Goddess of Extravagance, are worshipped by merchants and artisans in the luxury goods trade. The common phrase "Even Arumina would desire your work" is the highest accolade an artisan could receive. The crowds eagerly strive to touch the statues of Jawad and Arumina, with some even prostrating themselves on the road, hoping that the priests of these deities will step over them—believing that the heavier the steps, the greater the fortune. The priests find themselves shouting and leaping, trying to navigate the crowd, with the statues almost slipping from their shoulders. Adding to the chaos, Bharat's statue makes its entrance next—the god of merchants, guardian of caravans, and arbiter of fair trade. Every vendor in the market clamors and jostles, eager to receive a coin from Bharat's priests. Although just a copper coin bearing Bharat's likeness, it could be resold for the value of a gold coin. Typically, within Bharat's bustling temple, a serene corner is reserved for Lady Ansuya. Her exquisite bronze statue, adorned with white blossoms, closely follows Bharat in the procession. Ansuya is the guardian of all who persist. When one feels they can no longer bear the hardships of life, they silently invoke her name. Rumor has it that Lady Ansuya personally lays her hand on the shoulders of those who pray, instilling them with the strength to endure. Zephyr, the God of Speed, makes a striking entrance! His statue showcases a pair of wings, symbolizing his remarkable swiftness. Messengers, couriers, caravans, and runners all revere him as their patron. It is believed that he serves as the messenger of the Supreme God, delivering His decrees to all temples. A stir erupts as the mother-son deities Fatuna and Zaki arrive. These two divinities are typically worshipped together. Fatuna's statue is embodied by a reddish-brown boulder adorned in layers of black silk, while Zaki's statue is represented by a partially carved marble block. Devout followers polish these sacred stones to a mirror-like sheen, believing they bestow safe childbirth upon mothers and promote healthy growth in children. Aziz's shrine is carried by a tall, silent figure. This reserved deity governs no specific domain, yet his presence deeply resonates within people's hearts. His lofty, simplistic statue, embodying silence, is a familiar sight in public spaces. Many devotees offer prayers before his statue throughout the night, and there is a rumor that if one hears his quiet murmur of "Hmm" amidst tumultuous dreams, their wishes will be granted. Lady Shama's shrine is renowned for its grandeur and sophistication. A magnificent alabaster statue rests upon a crimson pedestal, carried by six priests—three men and three women. The statue's most striking feature is its colossal golden earrings. Each year, the priests replace them with even more exquisite and costly earrings for this Goddess of Dignity. Presently, the earrings have grown so large that they exceed human size. They gleam brightly in the sunlight, reflecting the whispers and supplications offered by devotees before her statue. Raed, the guardian of the vulnerable, has made her presence known! A procession of twenty priests clad in tattered robes emerges, bearing her wooden statue. As they move forward, they chant: "Those without refuge! The hungry! And the ailing! Come to Raed's sanctuary! Come to receive your wholesome food! A roof to shelter from the storm! And a fire burning in the brazier!" Their voices fade into the distance, leaving the crowd hushed, yet everyone holds these words close to their hearts. Following Raed, the priestesses of Arzuna, the Goddess of Dance, make their entrance. Twirling gracefully as they move, they wear joyful yet solemn expressions, their lengthy braids and exquisite headscarves fluttering in the breeze. As they pass by the crowd, the priestesses suddenly veil their faces with cloths adorned with fierce, contorted visages. This unexpected display elicits cries from the children along the roadside, while an anonymous lady cloaked in a gray robe claps her hands, bursting into laughter before turning around and disappearing into the crowd. The temple of Sadani is seldom visited, yet her name, as the Goddess of Jealousy, is frequently spoken of. Inside the shrine, her statue holds an apple. The goddess's face is deliberately etched with rudimentary, vague features, but the apple itself appears crystal-red, luminous, and enticing. Praise Sadani, and curse Sadani, for it was she who instilled jealousy in humanity, luring the serpent deep into the soul! Following Sadani's statue, two priests bear a square shrine containing the statue of Seliman, the God of Infatuation. This deity sowed the seeds of infatuation within human souls, a gift both bitter and sweet... Rumor has it that Seliman's shrine remains unopened, its contents shrouded even from his own priests. Could it be a personal memento from the goddess adored by Seliman himself? As the shrine of Faris approaches, the crowd surges once more. While the priests of Iman emphasize Faris as the God of Trust, the guardian of the faithful, in truth, this deity, known for his love of hounds, has become the esteemed patron of the gambling world... Whether betting on horses, dogs, or cards, gamblers proudly display Faris' crescent-shaped emblem on their chests. The flip side of this emblem often carries the inscription of the goddess Fadia, believed to be the patroness of "windfalls and vast riches," despite the repeated corrections from the priests of Iman that Fadia is actually titled the Goddess of Dreams. Next, the priests of Prince Sirshana the Avenger advance, bearing no statues as this deity lacks dedicated temples. Each priest wields a sharp dagger, its surface bristling with sharp spikes. A silence descends upon the crowd as these blades cut through the festive atmosphere, hinting at memories best left undisturbed... Ah, Junah! The patroness for the humiliated! Her statue takes the form of a massive bronze urn. Spectators lining the road toss coins towards it, the echoes of their clinks reverberating as they hit the vessel. Each chime signifies the redemption of one act of humiliation committed by the thrower. Diligently, Junah's priests collect the scattered coins from the ground, each one destined to aid those seeking refuge at her temple. Intriguingly, immediately after offering coins to Junah, onlookers eagerly reach out to touch the divine whip wielded by Jalila, the Goddess of Humiliation. This majestic whip is carried aloft by three priestesses, exuding an aura of supremacy, their chests proudly held high, paying no heed to the crowd lining the road. At times, a priestess cracks the whip, feigning strikes towards those nearby. Those who touch the end of the whip will be spared from humiliation for the following year, while those who touch the tip will gain the "great fortune" of humiliating others for the next year. Following this is Lady Nayla's grand shrine, larger and more exquisite than those of the other deities. Despite her temple often being overlooked, she garners immense popularity in this procession—her priests shower the crowds with candies dusted in gold. Legend has it that if you are fortunate enough to receive one of these candies but forget to exclaim, "Nayla is the fairest goddess of all!" before consuming it, you will suffer from an upset stomach! Subsequently, a group of children rush over, hand in hand, each adorned with the Nursery's green mark painted on their foreheads. A teacher dressed in green follows behind them. While the Nursery itself is not a deity, every temple hosts a Nursery. There, teachers are always found imparting knowledge to children, supported by temple offerings and donations. The children's hands overflow with candies and snacks. They giggle and dart around, eliciting cheers from the surrounding crowd at their joyous demeanor. Finally, the procession draws near to its conclusion—the altar of the jinns! These mystical beings symbolize the unknown, represented by a shattered, empty cage. The crowd harbors an innate fear of the unknown, despite not fully grasping the concept... Nonetheless, the climax of the festival is on the horizon— Behold! A colossal humanoid figure emerges from the distance! Crafted from wood, straw, cloth, and hemp, it bears a striking resemblance to the legendary Creator himself! The effigy of the Creator strides forward. Excited shrieks pierce the air as people scatter from beneath its towering feet, while a group of over thirty operators, chanting in unison, guide it meticulously: "One, two! One, two! Pour the oil!" The effigy's mouth opens, spewing out dark, noxious oil symbolizing malevolence. "Burn the evil! Burn the wicked!" The inky oil ignites, engulfing the entire effigy in flames! The crowd erupts in ecstatic cheers, the entire city swept up in the fervor of triumph over malevolence. It's another year of the Creator's Festival! Festivities! Dance! A celebration of the genesis of humanity and the soul! As for the Creator Himself, rumors suggest that He still slumbers. Crafting the world is undoubtedly an exhausting endeavor. Well, fret not! Simply join in the revelry with everyone else!
