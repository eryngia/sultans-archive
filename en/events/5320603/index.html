Text:

"I heard you have built a fine guesthouse!" Jenna cried joyfully one day. "Please—let me help there! I want to see how most of this city dresses itself." It was a most unusual request—many nobles would have called such an arrangement an insult—but Jenna was different. She flitted happily through the guesthouse, bringing people food and gathering dishes for the kitchen; she wore different jewels every day, dazzling the retainers' eyes—though of course no one would be stupid enough to attempt robbery in your guesthouse. From the second day on, women from the slums often came to see Jenna. They usually cradled children in one arm and clutched their work in the other—spinning, handiwork, or a beggar's bowl—and gazed longingly at the ornaments. Jenna told them gently that many of the stones she used were not expensive at all, yet they looked no worse than the costly gems nobles favored. Her goal was to become the best jeweler in the city, so she would certainly accept orders from the poor—after all, it was the hands of laborers that pushed this city forward. The women's eyes shone. They understood her.
