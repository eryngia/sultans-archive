Text:

You told the young man that when you do good deeds, you never expect a return from others, but that doesn't mean you don't need a return. He apologized for his dullness and hoped you could explain further. "You don't have to repay my kindness; you should pass it on to others, and thus, goodwill will ripple around us like a stone causing ripples on the water. In the end, these ripples will always come back to me, not from you, not from him, perhaps not from anyone I know, because the world has changed because of our good deeds, and I will eventually benefit from it." He looked at you with wide eyes, and after a long time, he knelt down before you, swearing to follow you for life. You could tell that your words benefited him greatly.

Text:

You told the young man that promptly repaying the kindness of others is as important as taking revenge on malice because only then will acts of kindness be encouraged and repeated over and over again. He apologized for his recklessness and then asked you if doing good with the expectation of receiving some reward could still count as a good deed? You replied to him that it certainly counts as a good deed because the final decision of receiving a reward still depends on the willingness of the receiving party. The doer of good has done a definite good deed and received an unknown reward. This kind of unequal exchange, what else can it be if not 'kindness'? The young man was greatly inspired, he pondered for a long time, and suddenly thought of something, taking out a jewelry box from his pocket. "My mother asked me to bring this to you as a reward for your teaching, hoping it can motivate you to continue teaching more people what kindness and nobility are."
