Text:

You meet Samir again under the eaves. The royal guards have already disposed of his father's body. There is no lightness of acquittal on his face—only fatigue and confusion. "I don't understand my father." He frowns, muttering low. "Was he trying to save me? Or covering for those of our people who plotted against me, cleaning their last mess?" It seems he had long suspected who framed him, but never had the chance to confirm it. "Honestly, I'm not surprised. Who else would hate me so fiercely as my former kin? I betrayed my homeland, accepted the Sultan'a patron; I dragged our name through the mud and shamed them! But what did they ever give me? For their stubborn 'loyalty' I could no longer be a noble, nor a physician, nor even hold my head high as a man—we were as poor as dogs by the roadside. What noble would hire the doctor of a fallen court? Which commoner could afford the fee of a 'Court Physician'?" "Yet I still wanted to be a doctor. I crawled my way out of that mire, went to the Chamberlain, declared I was of the fallen kingdom's court physicians and would forfeit all to serve the Sultan..." He buries his face in his hands. "Yes, they should hate me. I know. Sometimes, I hate this part of myself too."

"My father..." Samir drew a long breath and steadied himself. "Before the fallen kingdom fell—praise be to the Sultan!—the last order my father received from the king was to compile a new medical codex. From my earliest memories he was doing this, and he always trained me to do the same: compiling entries, writing annotations... After we fled that house, I never picked up the pen again, but now I wanted to finish it, so that I would owe him nothing further."
