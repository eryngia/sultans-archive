Title:

Schrödinger's Morality

Text:

You recall a mischievous philosopher who imagined a cat sealed in a box with poison - without opening the box, one cannot know whether the cat triggered the poison and died; for the observer, the cat exists simultaneously alive and dead. Morality resembles this paradox. An action is simultaneously virtuous and wicked, depending on who observes; morality both exists and doesn't exist, depending on whether you currently need it. Understanding this brings unprecedented freedom... Nothing constrains you anymore. You've placed your conscience in that box, sharing space with the cat. The Cultic God apparently appreciate your metaphor, including the cat portion - sending a hot wind carrying complex scents of soil, meat, and animal musk, clearly conveying: everything predates morality, rendering morality meaningless.

Intimate Lash

You think of Faris. Doesn't he regularly whip his hunting hounds? Yet they depend on him, trust him, view him as their sole master... Do these hounds follow morality? Are they born more loyal and noble than the finest aristocrats? Hardly! They merely follow animal instinct, which we misinterpret as "morality"! Isn't everything similar? Humans are merely naked apes, our so-called morality merely survival strategies evolved through instinct? As you contemplate this, darkness seems to produce soft laughter... Something appreciates your reasoning.
